# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Marketing teams and social media agencies managing LinkedIn content for multiple brand accounts or clients across LATAM countries (Colombia, Argentina, Peru, Chile, Mexico). They open this tool to plan, schedule, and track what goes live and when, across which market, without losing the thread of regional intent.

## Product Purpose

A LinkedIn publication calendar built specifically for LATAM content teams. It makes cross-country content planning visible at a glance: what's scheduled, for which country, and when. Success looks like a team that never misses a market, never double-books a week, and ships regional content with confidence.

## Brand Personality

Clean · Confident · LATAM. The tool is built for this region, not adapted from somewhere else. Warmth and identity come through the country flags, regional color cues, and purposeful details — not from loud UI. The interface stays out of the way; the content plan is the hero.

## Anti-references

- **Buffer, Hootsuite**: bloated, SaaS-generic, feels like enterprise software from 2018. Cluttered chrome, aggressive CTAs, low visual confidence.
- **HubSpot, Canva**: marketing-heavy UI, too much color, too many tooltips, too many gradients. Treats the user like they need hand-holding.
- **Monday.com, Asana**: corporate productivity aesthetic — flat, cheerful, completely devoid of editorial sensibility.

## Design Principles

1. **Regional identity is structural, not decorative** — country flags and color cues are load-bearing UI, not emoji sprinkled on top. The calendar should read as designed for LATAM.
2. **The plan is the product** — the calendar grid is the UI. Navigation, controls, and chrome exist only to serve it. Nothing competes with the month view.
3. **White-surface confidence** — clean backgrounds with deliberate typographic hierarchy, à la Notion/Vercel. No noise, no gradients, no decorative complexity. Precision spacing and strong contrast do the work.
4. **Both modes feel native** — dark mode is not an afterthought. Light and dark each get their own visual logic, not just an inverted palette.
5. **Tool-grade reliability over marketing polish** — interactions are snappy, states are clear, every affordance has a clear label. This is a working tool for professionals, not a product demo.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components. Keyboard navigable (modal traps focus, Escape closes). Reduced motion respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`. Color is never the sole means of conveying country information (flag emoji + label always present).
